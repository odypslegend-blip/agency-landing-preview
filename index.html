A clear process. Zero guesswork.

Four phases, plain-language deliverables at each one. Timelines are scoped per project after discovery; we don’t quote before we understand.

Tell us what you’re building

  1. Discover

    A short, structured conversation about your business, customers and goals. We look at what you have, what your competitors assume, and what “better” means for you.

    Ships: Goals & scope notes · Written quote

  2. Direction

    We propose a visual and verbal direction (typography, palette, structure) before anything is built, so you approve the feel early, not after the invoice.

    Ships: Art direction · Page structure / wireframes

  3. Design & build

    The approved direction becomes real pages: design system, content laid in, motion with restraint, and a build that passes performance and accessibility checks.

    Ships: Full design · Production build · Device & browser QA

  4. Launch & improve

    Launch checklist, search-ready metadata, analytics where agreed; then a review cycle once real visitors arrive. Care options continue from there if you want them.

    Ships: Launch checklist · Handover documentation · Improvement round

Fair questions.

What happens after I email?

We read every enquiry ourselves and reply personally. Expect a short set of questions about your goals, scope and timing, and a call if it looks like a fit. No sales sequence, no newsletter unless you ask for one.

Can you redesign my existing site?

Yes: redesigns and content migrations are a core offer. We audit what’s working (pages, search visibility, content worth keeping) before proposing changes, so a redesign improves the site instead of just restyling it.

How long does a project take?

It depends on scope, content readiness and decision speed, so we don’t quote a timeline before discovery. After the first conversation you get a written scope and schedule, and we stick to both.

Do you only build websites, or marketing too?

Both. Websites and brand identity are the core; search and campaign work is offered as defined scopes with agreed deliverables: measurement set up properly, not vague monthly activity.

What should I include in a first email?

Three things are enough: what your business does, what you want the site (or campaign) to achieve, and any deadline you’re working against. A link to your current site helps if you have one.
